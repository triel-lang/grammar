\title{A Mechanised Core of the TRIEL Specification Language}
\author{Dmitri Chistyakov}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This document is a core of the semantics of the TRIEL specification language,
formalised and checked in Isabelle/HOL. It is based only on the public description of
the language (\texttt{TECHNICAL\_REPORT.md}, Sections 2.5--2.9, and
\texttt{FOUNDATIONS.md}), in version 2.5.1, the errata to version 2.5. All proofs are complete: there are no unproved lemmas and no
axioms beyond those of Isabelle/HOL.
\end{abstract}

\section*{What is formalised}

\paragraph{Expressions (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Core}).}
States are partial maps from names to values, ordered by information (extension).
Boolean expressions are evaluated in strong Kleene three-valued logic~\cite{Kleene1938}; the value-presence
predicate \texttt{PRESENT(x)} is always defined, and a comparison $=$, $<$ or $\le$ with
an operand that has no value is undefined. Proved: commutativity and associativity
of \texttt{AND} and \texttt{OR}, De Morgan's laws and double negation (T1); monotonicity of
every \texttt{PRESENT}-free expression under extension of the state, the claim of
Section~2.9 (T2; cf.\ the strong Kleene connectives of the logic
LPF~\cite{BarringerChengJones1984} and the monotone valuation schemes of
Kripke~\cite{Kripke1975}); non-monotonicity of \texttt{PRESENT} (T3); agreement with two-valued
logic when every name read is defined (T4); the meaning of \texttt{PRESENT} (T5).

\paragraph{The indicator predicate (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Ex}).}
Following the logics of quasiary predicates of Nikitchenko and
Shkilniak~\cite{NikitchenkoShkilniak2017,ShkilniakShkilniak2024}, a predicate is
given by its truth and falsity domains, and the total indicator predicate $E_x$ is true
where $x$ has a value and false where it has none. Proved: \texttt{PRESENT(x)} is $E_x$
(\texttt{present\_is\_Ex}); $E_x$ is total and not equitone; $E_x$ holds exactly when the
value of $x$ equals some value, as for the existence predicate of free logic; every
\texttt{PRESENT}-free expression is equitone; the comparison $x = x$ is the partial
indicator predicate.

\paragraph{Trace semantics (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Trace}).}
Traces are non-empty sequences of (state, timestamp, event) entries with non-decreasing
timestamps; executions end \texttt{Done}, \texttt{Interrupted} or \texttt{Violated}. The
denotations of \texttt{MUST}, \texttt{MAY}, \texttt{MUST\_NOT}, \texttt{THEN},
\texttt{AND}, \texttt{OR} and \texttt{UNLESS} follow Section~2.5. An event is matched by
subject and action; the actions of a term are the events of the entries after the one
where it is reached, so a trace of \texttt{MUST} has at least two entries; a prohibition
completes at once and is violated by a trace that ends with its first forbidden action.
Proved: the laws of
\texttt{UNLESS} --- \texttt{t UNLESS false DO e = t}, distributivity over \texttt{OR},
distributivity over \texttt{THEN} for a total right operand, and idempotence; a
counterexample showing that distributivity over \texttt{THEN} fails without totality; the
inequality of nested \texttt{UNLESS} and \texttt{UNLESS} with a disjunctive guard.

\paragraph{Terms (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Terms}).}
Terms form an inductive type with a compositional denotation. Proved: every term is total
(\texttt{den\_total}), and therefore all four laws of \texttt{UNLESS}, including
distributivity over \texttt{THEN}, hold for every term without side conditions. Two
obligations in sequence need two actions, and a prohibition inside a composite term can
end it \texttt{Violated}; the evaluator reports the status of a composite term, read on
the first prefix of the trace that violates the term, or that completes it by an executed
action while no reading of it or of an earlier prefix has reached a prohibition, and this
status is final. For a term without \texttt{AND}, a violated prefix is reported as a
violation at that entry or before it, unless the status is unknown or the observation has
already ended on a completion by an executed action, before any reading of a prefix had
reached a prohibition (\texttt{status\_no\_missed\_violation}); the exception for an earlier
completion cannot be dropped (\texttt{violation\_after\_completion}).

\paragraph{Nominative data (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_ND}).}
Following the composition-nominative approach of Nikitchenko~\cite{Nikitchenko2022} and the
Mizar formalisation of simple-named complex-valued nominative data by Ivanov, Nikitchenko,
Kryvolap and Korni{\l}owicz~\cite{IvanovNikitchenkoKryvolapKornilowicz2017}, a datum is a basic value or a finite partial
map from names to data; records and nested structures are such data, and an optional
value is a name that may be absent. Expressions use complex names (paths), and
\texttt{PRESENT(p)} is the total indicator predicate for a complex name. The information
order allows names to be added at any depth. Proved: it is a partial order; flattening to a
state over complex names is monotone, so the monotonicity of Section~2.9 holds for nested
data; the counterparts of T1--T5; \texttt{PRESENT(p)} is $E_p$; typing of open records is
monotone for types without \texttt{Optional}, and a counterexample shows that it is not
monotone with \texttt{Optional}.

\paragraph{Deadlines and breach (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Breach}).}
Following Section~2.6, implementation traces with integer timestamps are given verdicts:
an obligation \texttt{BY} or \texttt{WITHIN} a deadline is fulfilled, breached or pending;
a prohibition is breached when its condition is true before the action; a permission is
never breached. Deadlines are fixed at activation. Proved: the violated traces of a
prohibition are exactly the prefixes up to its breach; a missed deadline is a breach;
verdicts are final once reached; fail-closed enforcement of prohibitions blocks every
breach and also undefined conditions, which the verdict does not count as breaches (cf.\
the enforcement of security policies by execution monitors~\cite{Schneider2000}); breach
handlers bind to the nearest preceding obligation or prohibition of the same subject; at most one
continuation-determining action is allowed; a cure in time prevents a second breach, and a
failed cure is final; a result computed with stale factors treated as absent is not
overturned when they are refreshed.

\paragraph{Invariants (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Invariants}).}
\texttt{ALWAYS}, \texttt{EVENTUALLY} and \texttt{NEXT} are evaluated on finite prefixes of
implementation traces with the three verdicts of LTL3 (Bauer, Leucker and
Schallhart~\cite{BauerLeuckerSchallhart2011}).
Proved: on a prefix \texttt{ALWAYS} is never true and \texttt{EVENTUALLY} never false; a
definite verdict is final under extension; an entry where the formula is undefined for
lack of data neither violates \texttt{ALWAYS} nor fulfils \texttt{EVENTUALLY}; definite
verdicts survive more data.

\paragraph{Indicator predicates (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Indicators}).}
Following Shkilniak (2024)~\cite{Shkilniak2024}, an R-predicate is given by a truth domain and a falsity domain,
and a P-predicate is one whose two domains are disjoint. Besides the total indicator $E_z$
(true where $z$ has a value, false where it has none) there is the partial indicator
$\downarrow z$, true where $z$ has a value and never false. Proved: $E_z$ and
$\downarrow z$ have the same truth domain; $\downarrow z$ is an equitone, irrefutable
P-predicate, and in TRIEL it is the comparison $z = z$; $E_z$ is total and single-valued
but not monotone, with an explicit counterexample; partial presence of a complex name is
monotone under extension of nominative data. A condition built from equitone atoms,
such as $\downarrow z$, by \texttt{NOT}, \texttt{AND} and \texttt{OR} keeps every
definite verdict when the data is extended, comparisons included; with the atom $E_z$ this fails, on the data of
the counterexample for typing with \texttt{Optional}. The equitone P-predicates are
closed under negation and disjunction.

\paragraph{Compositions (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Compositions}).}
Following Nikitchenko and Shkilniak (2017)~\cite{NikitchenkoShkilniak2017} and Shkilniak
(2024)~\cite{Shkilniak2024}, the existential
quantifier and renomination are defined on R-predicates. Renomination is traditional or
extended; an extended renomination may also delete names. Proved: the renomination
operation is monotone; the classes of P-predicates, of monotone R-predicates and of
equitone P-predicates are closed under all base compositions (negation, disjunction,
renomination, the quantifier and $\downarrow z$), as stated by Shkilniak (2024)~\cite{Shkilniak2024}; $E_z$ is
the negation of the variable unassignment predicate of Nikitchenko and
Shkilniak~\cite{NikitchenkoShkilniak2017}.
Formulas of the language are terms of the composition algebra, interpreted by the
compositions. Every condition of \texttt{TRIEL\_Indicators} translates to such a term,
with \texttt{AND} expressed by negation and disjunction, and the value of the condition is
the value of the term. The stability of definite verdicts follows again, from the closure.

\paragraph{Numbers (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Numbers}).}
There is no floating point. An Integer is a rational number in $\mathbb{Z}$, and a Decimal
is a rational number $m / 10^k$. Proved: every Integer is a Decimal; a rational number in
lowest terms is a Decimal if and only if its denominator divides a power of 10, that is,
has no prime factor other than 2 and 5; $1/3$ is not a Decimal. Both types are decided by
executable equations.

\paragraph{Penalties (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Penalty}).}
Following Sections~2.6 and 2.9, the amount of \texttt{PENALTY e CAP c} is built from
values with $+$, $-$ and $\times$, without division, and computed on exact numbers; it is
undefined when an operand is undefined or not a number. The amount of an obligation is
read in the last state not after its deadline, and that of a prohibition in the state
before the forbidden action, in which its condition is true. Proved: an amount is
undefined when an operand is; without \texttt{DEFAULT} a defined amount is not changed by
more data, and with \texttt{DEFAULT} it can be; the Decimals are closed under $+$, $-$
and $\times$; an amount reported for payment is never negative and, with a non-negative
cap, never exceeds the cap (\texttt{pen\_amount\_bounds}), while a negative amount is
reported as negative and not replaced by 0; once a norm
is breached, later entries change neither the state of the breach nor the reported
actions and amounts.

\paragraph{The evaluator (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Exec}).}
Executable equations, each proved equal to a definition of the other theories, replace
unbounded quantifiers, \texttt{LEAST} and \texttt{GREATEST}. Membership of a trace in the
denotation of a composite term is decided three-valued: definite answers are proved
sound for terms without \texttt{AND}, and are always given for terms without
\texttt{UNLESS} and \texttt{AND}. Stale factors are blocked by factor, and the safety
claim of Section~2.9 is proved again for this blocking. The function \texttt{run}
computes the report of a specification on an implementation trace and is exported to
Haskell. It rejects a list of observations whose times decrease, and for every list of
observations a decided verdict of its report stays the same when observations are
appended, unless the longer list is rejected for the order of its times or for the types
of its data (\texttt{run\_final}). A breached prohibition is reported with the informational actions of the handler
bound to it; a continuation in such a handler is rejected, since Section~2.6 defines
continuations only for obligations.

\paragraph{Hidden factors (theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Exec}).}
A factor of type \texttt{ZK<T>} without \texttt{WITHOUT REVEALING ALL} is hidden, and
\texttt{run} removes every hidden factor from the data of every entry before it
evaluates. Proved: two lists of observations that are equal once the hidden factors are
removed get the same report, typing included (\texttt{run\_noninterference}), so the
report is the same with or without the hidden values in the data (\texttt{run\_erase}); a
hidden factor is never reported as ill-typed; an expression has the same value in two
states that agree on the names it reads, so a condition that reads no hidden factor, as
the flow rule of Section~2.7 requires, has the same value at every entry with the hidden
factors removed and with them left in (\texttt{flow\_safe\_eval}). The flow rule itself is
checked by the translator.

\paragraph{Related entries of the Archive of Formal Proofs.}
The entry \texttt{LTL3\_Semantics} by Amjad, van Glabbeek and
O'Connor~\cite{LTL3_Semantics-AFP} formalises the full logic LTL3, with a semantics by
definitive prefix sets and formula progression. Theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Invariants} has
only \texttt{ALWAYS}, \texttt{EVENTUALLY} and \texttt{NEXT} over a state formula, which is
evaluated in strong Kleene logic on incomplete data. The entry \texttt{MFOTL\_Monitor} by
Schneider and Traytel~\cite{MFOTL_Monitor-AFP} verifies an executable monitor for metric
first-order temporal logic and exports its code; it is the closest counterpart of the
evaluator of theory \texttt{TRIEL\_Exec}.

\paragraph{Not formalised.}
Arithmetic occurs only in the amount of a \texttt{PENALTY}; elsewhere the translator
rejects it, as it rejects function calls, \texttt{LET} and the \texttt{IF} expression. A
deadline or \texttt{FROM} inside a composite term is rejected, since the trace semantics
of Section~2.5 has no time, and so is \texttt{WITHIN \dots\ ELSE} inside a composite term.
The composition of terms by \texttt{AND} is defined with interleaving as a parameter, and
neither the evaluator nor the translator accepts it. \texttt{EXECUTE} and event-triggered
terms (\texttt{ON \dots\ DO}) are not accepted. Also outside the core: calendar units
(\texttt{BUSINESS\_DAY}, \texttt{MONTH}, \texttt{YEAR}), the types \texttt{List} and
\texttt{Map}, LTL formulas beyond \texttt{ALWAYS}, \texttt{EVENTUALLY} and \texttt{NEXT},
and CTL formulas. The translator from TRIEL to the core is not verified; it is checked
by tests.
